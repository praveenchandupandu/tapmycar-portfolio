Real scenarios

This happens every day.

These aren't rare events. They happen to thousands of car owners daily — and most of them never find out until it's too late.

Someone hits your parked car.

You're in a meeting. A delivery truck clips your mirror. The driver looks around — nobody's there. No way to contact you. They drive away. You come back to a damaged car and zero information about who did it.

Your headlights are on all night.

You forgot to turn them off. Your neighbor notices at 11pm. They want to tell you, but they don't know which apartment you're in. By morning, your battery is completely dead.

You're blocking someone's driveway.

You parked for "just 5 minutes" to grab coffee. But the line is long. Meanwhile, someone needs to get out of their driveway for an emergency. They can't reach you. Frustration builds. They call a tow truck.

Your car alarm is going off.

It's 2am in a parking garage. Your car alarm triggered — maybe wind, maybe someone tried to break in. Nobody in the building knows whose car it is. It rings for 45 minutes before security disables it. You never knew.

A child or pet is locked inside.

A bystander sees a dog panting in a hot car. They want to find the owner before calling police. But there's no way to identify who owns the car. Precious minutes are wasted. This is a life-or-death situation.

The hidden risk

The "solution" most people use is actually dangerous.

Right now, millions of people leave their phone number on a piece of paper on their dashboard. They think they're being responsible. But here's what they don't realize:

No way to screen who's calling. No log of who saw your number. No way to turn it off. No privacy whatsoever.
